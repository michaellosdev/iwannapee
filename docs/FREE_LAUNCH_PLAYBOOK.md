# IWANNAPEE free launch playbook

## The growth loop already built into the product

1. Invite a Glendale restaurant, cafe, bar, shop, or venue to claim its restroom listing for free.
2. Verify ownership manually in the owner dashboard after proof arrives at `iwannapee@proton.me`.
3. Approval creates a public business profile, community-verifies the restroom, and activates one complimentary seven-day placement.
4. Ask the business to print the listing's Community Verified QR badge, place it near the entrance or restroom, link the listing from its website, and share the card once.
5. Visitors scan the badge, verify information, add photos or reviews, and discover other listings. Every verified business becomes a distribution point.

Do not promise permanent free advertising. The free item is one seven-day launch placement. The claimed listing and QR badge remain free while the profile follows the site policies.

## First 30 days

### Days 1–3: make Google discover the directory

- Deploy and live-test `/sitemap.xml` and `/restroom-sitemaps.xml` without authentication.
- Add both URLs in Google Search Console's Sitemaps report, then inspect and request indexing for the homepage, Glendale city page, restroom directory, and two strong restroom pages.
- Check Page Indexing weekly. Google says sitemap submission helps discovery but does not guarantee or instantly produce indexing.
- Add a visible link to `/restrooms` from the homepage navigation or footer so the directory is discoverable without JavaScript interaction.

Official reference: <https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7451001>

### Days 4–10: recruit the first 25 Glendale partners

Start with independent businesses where the owner or manager is reachable. Visit during a quiet time or send this:

> Hi — I built IWANNAPEE, a community restroom map launching in Glendale. I would like to verify your restroom listing for free. You get a public business profile, a printable Community Verified QR badge, simple visit analytics, and one free seven-day featured placement. There is no card required. If it is useful, I only ask that you display the QR or share the listing once. Claim it here: [claim URL]

For every approval, send the exact public listing link and ask for three actions:

- print the QR badge;
- add the listing link to the business website or link-in-bio;
- publish one Google Business Profile update or offer pointing to the page.

Google Business Profile currently supports posts with photos, videos, action links, and offer codes: <https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169>

### Days 11–20: local community distribution

- Create a free Nextdoor Business Page and post a useful Glendale restroom-access guide, not a generic advertisement. Nextdoor currently advertises free business pages and unlimited free posts: <https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/getting-started/business-page>
- Ask Glendale libraries, parks, family groups, disability-access groups, delivery-driver groups, running clubs, parent groups, and mutual-aid organizers to verify a short list of locations.
- Contact local reporters and newsletters with the public-interest angle: restroom access, accessibility, parents, delivery workers, and community-verified imported data.
- Post to relevant local Reddit and Facebook groups only when their rules permit self-promotion. Lead with the useful Glendale directory and ask members to correct it; do not paste the same promotional message across groups.
- Publish one short vertical video per verified location: entrance, how to find the restroom, accessibility facts, and the live verification card. Never film people inside a restroom.

### Days 21–30: repeat what produces verified users

- Publish a weekly “Best verified restrooms in Glendale” page or social post based only on real ratings and verification data.
- Give each partner its appearance, listing-open, QR-copy, promo-copy, and website-click totals from the existing business dashboard.
- Ask the five most active partners to introduce one neighboring business.
- Turn common searches into useful city/editorial pages only when the content is genuinely distinct; do not create thin keyword pages.

## Metrics that matter

- verified business claims;
- QR scans or listing visits from partner links;
- community confirmations per listing;
- first-time reviewers who return within 30 days;
- Search Console indexed restroom pages and non-branded search clicks;
- partner-referred visits that become a verification, photo, note, or review.

Do not optimize for raw imported pin count. Optimize for verified, recently useful locations.

## Mobile-app decision

Do not build a native app yet. The responsive web app already provides location search, camera uploads, share links, and installable-site basics without splitting engineering and moderation across iOS, Android, and web.

Revisit native development when at least two of these are true:

- 1,000 monthly active users in one metro;
- at least 25% of active users return in 30 days;
- users repeatedly request offline maps, push alerts, background location, or faster camera contribution;
- partner QR scans convert reliably into account creation or community actions;
- the web funnel and moderation operation are stable enough to support another client.

Until then, spend the same effort on data accuracy, indexable pages, partner acquisition, and a polished installable web experience.
